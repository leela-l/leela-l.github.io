5G: Is it Really a Death Wish?

You may have heard the term radiation when talking about sunrays, microwaves, X-rays, or wireless networks, but what is actually radiation? Are all of these dangerous cancer causing rays?

There has been widespread panic about the health risks of radiation over social media and the news, so much so that it has been seen to influence politics. The most significant example is 5G, which we will discuss and debunk.

The reason for the controversy is a large misspread of information about the harm radiation can cause. Posts quote scientific papers without properly understanding it, showing only snapshots of scientific knowledge. This portrays the health risks to be much greater than it really is. What is radiation?

Radiation is energy that moves from one place to the other through space or a medium - it is emitted or transmitted. It can be in the form of something called electromagnetic waves or in the form of high speed particles. Electromagnetic waves spread out like invisible ripples, we can't see it but we know it affects what it passes through.

All electromagnetic waves travel with the same speed, but have different frequencies and wavelengths, giving each wave different properties. Visible light is an example of electromagnetic waves, as well as microwaves which are used to heat up your food.

Very high frequency and short wavelength (upper end of ultraviolet radiation) waves can be ionising, meaning they can cause harm to life by damaging cells and even mutating DNA. In extreme cases, this can lead to cancer, or radiation poisoning.
