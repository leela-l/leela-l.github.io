Impacts of 5G Misinformation

The impacts of the misinformation surrounding 5G are vast, particularly in politics. People's fears continue to be surrounding health impacts, but also the conspiracy of 5G being used by government agencies for malicious purposes. It implies that 5G is used to ‘spy’ on the public. Particularly in the height of the pandemic, rumours circulated the globe that 5G was the cause of the spread of the Corona Virus.

Below are just a few key examples of how the misuse of information surrounding 5G has impacted politics, though the impact is worldwide.

Global Case Studies: 5G Misinformation

Around the world, misinformation surrounding 5G has influenced politics, public opinion, and public health decisions. Explore some real examples below.

Australia (2020) →

Social media-fueled anti-5G movements caused nationwide panic over small cell installations and higher frequencies. Despite widespread fear, scientific evidence confirmed that these frequencies do not pose health risks.

Italy (2022) →

Political parties, including Alleanza Italiana Stop 5G, formalized public fears about 5G for political gain, appealing to voters distrustful of government and science.

Belgium →

Political pressure delayed 5G rollout, even without scientific support. Public concern over radiation led to regulatory hesitation, highlighting how misinformation can influence policy.

Switzerland →

Expert reports concluded 5G is not proven harmful, but unclear exposure limits caused public panic. This led to political debate and public pressure rather than decisions based on science.

Worldwide COVID-19 & 5G Panic →

During the pandemic, false claims linked 5G to virus spread through microchips in vaccines. These myths discouraged vaccination, worsening the impact of COVID-19. Viruses cannot travel via electromagnetic waves, and many countries without 5G still experienced outbreaks.
