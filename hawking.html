What is Hawking radiation?

Proposed in 1974 by Stephen Hawking, Hawking radiation solved the question of how black holes can decay. It is the theoretical prediction that black holes slowly emit thermal radiation due to quantum effects at the event horizon.

It turns out that empty space is not really ‘empty’. Pairs of virtual particles and their corresponding antiparticles are constantly coming into existence and then annihilating each other.

However, when this phenomenon occurs at the edge of a black hole, one particle can fall in, leaving the other to escape. To an outside observer, it appears as if the black hole emitted a particle. To conserve mass, a particle escaping dictates that the black hole must lose mass. As a result, black holes shrink over large timescales.

Hawking predicts that the radiation has a spectrum such that:

T ∝ 1 / M

Where T is the black hole’s temperature and M is its mass.

Essentially:

Small black holes → Hotter
Large black holes → Colder
