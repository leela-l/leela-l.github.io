Why should we care?

Wether you care about it or not, climate change is already affecting the world we live in. You might think its an issue for future generations but the impact is already being felt around the world and its consequences are only going to get worse. Click below to learn how climate change is transforming human life.

Consequences of Climate Change

Sea-level
Sea-level rise increases the risk of flooding and costal erosion. This has significant consequences for infrastructure, businesses and nature and could displace millions of people.

Sea-level is predicted to rise 60 to 80 cm by the end of the century. Around a third of the EU’s population lives within 50km of the coast, with these areas generating over 30% of the Union’s GDP.

In the US, Miami and New York are both vulnerable to costal flooding and storm surges, with high-tide flooding occurring more often every year. Jakarta, Indonesia, is sinking faster than any major city on Earth, with some areas dropping more than 10cm each year.

Food
Changing rainfall patterns disrupt agriculture causing varying crop yields. This leads to increased food prices and heightened food vulnerability for many populations worldwide.

India is projected to experience a 10% drop in rice and wheat production as a result of climate change, impacting millions of people. Viet Nam, the world’s second largest coffee supplier, saw a 20% drop in production. Coffee is one of five staples that together account for 40% of food inflation.

Prolonged droughts reduce freshwater supplies. This lowers the quality of our livestock and makes them more susceptible to diseases. Roughly 1 in 4 people lack access to safe drinking water, particularly in low income countries. These statistics will only worsen as climate change puts pressure on water supplies.

Biodiversity
Many species are struggling to adapt fast enough to the changing environment. For example coral reefs are dying due to ocean warming and acidification
Health
worsening of air quality leads to more respiratory problems and rising temperatures leave a higher risk of heat-realated illness and death
